# First AI lesson outline

This note is a lesson outline only. It does not add a route, a setting, a migration, or a live model call.

Masterasium math covers Grades 1–8. A parent signs up on their own device. A child signs in with a first name and a PIN. Math lives at `/practice`. The parent desk is `/dashboard`. Site copy says practice is free while we test. This note has no prices.

AI is its own section, with its own top-level tab. It is not a step on the math grade ladder. Every AI lesson carries an age line, "Suggested for age X and up," so a family can choose. This first lesson is anchored at Grade 8 and tagged **Suggested for age 13 and up**. A later capstone is tagged **Suggested for age 15 and up**. High school math is a later build. It is not part of this section.

The aim of this first lesson is to teach a careful way to use AI. AI can help a student research, brainstorm, and check. It is not an answer key. Copying a chatbot and handing that in is the habit this lesson pushes back on.

The AI tab is always in the header. Lesson pages stay locked until a parent turns AI on from the parent desk. The toggle starts off. When it is on, this section is the one place a child talks to a live model. Math practice never calls a live model. That rule stays as it is.

House style for anything a child or parent sees: quiet and focused. No points, streaks, badges, confetti, timers, reward sounds, or mascots. Listen stays available. Tap targets are at least 48px. The main action fits a 1366×768 screen without scrolling. Minutes below are for planning. The child never sees a clock.

Buttons, titles, and feedback the child reads stay under 15 words. A sample paragraph used for fact-checking can be a few short sentences. That sample is labeled as a sample with mistakes.

## 1. Title and one-line description

**Title:** Using AI well

**Age line on the lesson card:** Suggested for age 13 and up

**One-line description:** Learn to use AI as a helper, then do the thinking yourself.

Kid-facing title under the age line: "You stay in charge."

---

## 2. Learning objectives

By the end, a student can:

1. Say, in their own words, that this tool predicts likely text or images from patterns, and that it can sound sure and still be wrong.
2. Write a prompt that asks for options or a check, and that leaves the final piece to the student.
3. Replace a weak follow-up ("Tell me more") with a question that asks for a reason, a limit, or a fact to check.
4. Mark a confident mistake in a sample answer and check it against two reviewed sources.
5. Produce a short final piece in their own words, with a process log that shows what the tool suggested and what they kept, changed, and checked.
6. Stop before sending a prompt that asks for sexual content, a slur, violence, self-harm, or personal information.

---

## 3. Step-by-step lesson flow

One pass is about 40 minutes. It splits into three sittings of about 12 to 14 minutes. Each segment has its own page, so Back and refresh return to that step. A quiet Continue button is the only primary action on the lesson home.

Nothing counts a score. Feedback is one calm sentence, in the same spirit as practice ("That's right." / "Not quite. Try again.").

The child's own sentences stay on the device. Planned device store name: `masterasium-ai`. It keeps the step and the typed words until Done, or for 14 days, whichever comes first. Create-mode drafts on the unmerged prototype expire the same Toronto day. That expiry is too short for a lesson split across sittings. This lesson should not reuse it.

No segment shows a minute count.

### Sitting 1 — about 12 minutes

**Start** (`/ai/using-ai-well/start`) — about 2 minutes

- Sees: the title, the age line, and three short lines. "This tool predicts likely words." "It can sound sure and still be wrong." "You still do the thinking."
- Sees: one Listen control for those lines. Same control as practice (`Listen` / `Stop`, device speech, no chime).
- Does: taps Continue.
- Does not see: a score, a timer, or a chat box yet.

**What it is** (`/ai/using-ai-well/what-it-is`) — about 4 minutes

- Sees: four plain lines, one screen, no scroll to the button.
  - "A model predicts the next likely words."
  - "It can also predict an image."
  - "It learned from patterns, not from your life."
  - "A sure tone is not proof."
- Sees: one picture of the idea, drawn as a simple diagram (patterns in, a likely reply out). No mascot.
- Does: taps "I can say this back" and types or builds one sentence from word chips: "It predicts. It can be wrong."
- Hears, if they tap Listen: the four lines, not the chips.
- Feedback if the sentence is empty: "Add one short sentence."
- Feedback when it is filled: "That is the idea."

**Sort** (`/ai/using-ai-well/sort`) — about 6 minutes

- Sees: the activity "Who might have written this?" Four short cards, one at a time, so the choice stays on screen.
- Does: taps "A person" or "A model" for each card. Full card text is in section 4.
- Does: reads the close, which does not mark the sort right or wrong. "Smooth words can still be wrong."
- End of sitting: "You can stop here." Continue saves the place. Leave returns to the AI lesson list. Math stays on its own tab.

### Sitting 2 — about 14 minutes

**Mistake** (`/ai/using-ai-well/mistake`) — about 6 minutes

- Sees: a fixed sample answer about blue whales, labeled "Sample answer. It has mistakes." The model is not called on this page. The mistakes are planted so the lesson cannot drift.
- Does: taps the two sentences that are wrong. Details and the repair lines are in section 4.
- Feedback: "It sounded sure. Two parts were wrong."
- Then sees the repair, in the student's path, before they leave the page. The false lines do not get the last word.

**Prompt** (`/ai/using-ai-well/prompt`) — about 4 minutes

- Sees: a weak prompt, "Write my poster for me." Under it: "This asks the tool to do your work."
- Sees: a better prompt the lesson offers, ready to edit. Text in section 4.
- Does: changes at least one limit (the topic chip, or the line "I will write the poster"). Taps Use this.
- Feedback: "Your prompt leaves the work with you."
- If they submit the weak line unchanged: "Add a limit. You write the final words." The page does not call a model.

**Follow-up** (`/ai/using-ai-well/follow-up`) — about 4 minutes

- Sees: three follow-up questions for the same poster. Weak, better, and strong. Text in section 4.
- Does: taps the strong one, then writes one follow-up of their own.
- Feedback when their question asks for a reason, a limit, or a check: "That question asks for a check."
- Feedback when it is only "tell me more" or very close to it: "Ask for a reason or a check."
- This page uses the fixed ladder. It does not call a model. The habit is the point.

### Sitting 3 — about 14 minutes

**Check** (`/ai/using-ai-well/check`) — about 5 minutes

- Sees: the same whale sample, plus two short source cards. The cards are reviewed paraphrases, not a live web search. The product does not open a browser.
- Does: taps which source disagrees with which sentence. Then taps "Both sources agree" under the repair lines.
- Feedback: "You checked two sources."
- A parent who wants the real pages can look them up later. The lesson still works with no extra tab.

**Own words** (`/ai/using-ai-well/own-words`) — about 4 minutes

- Sees: a short AI-style paragraph on saving water at school, labeled "Sample draft. Not yours yet." Draft text in section 4.
- Sees: an empty box, "Your version," with the button "This is mine."
- Does: writes two to four sentences. The button stays quiet until the text is not a copy of the sample.
- An internal check can compare the texts. The child never sees a percent.
- Feedback: "This is in your words."
- Prompt under the box: "What did you change?" One short answer is enough.

**Make** (`/ai/using-ai-well/make`) — about 5 minutes for a small finish

- Sees: five project cards. Job ad. Homepage. Superhero. Comic. Poster. Each card is one line. No thumbnails of real people or brands.
- Does: picks one. The small version is specified in section 5. A full studio session is not required.
- The project step may ask the live model for three short options. The model does not write the finished piece. Filters in section 6 run before the call and before the reply is shown. Teaching pages that use a planted mistake stay on fixed text.
- If the spend cap or the key blocks the call, the child sees "This help is not available right now." They can still finish in their own words.
- Does: fills the process log, writes the final lines themselves, and taps Done.
- Done screen: "You did the thinking." One line under it: "You can stop here." No share button. No public gallery.

Continue on the lesson home opens the first unfinished segment. Done clears the device draft. A parent can turn the section off from the parent desk at any time. Turning it off locks the AI tab for that child. Math practice is unchanged. The account stays.

## 4. Activities in detail

These five activities are the lesson. The project in section 5 is where the student uses the same habits on a small piece of their own.

### AI or human

One card at a time. The child taps "A person" or "A model." There is no score. The point is that the voice does not prove the author, and it does not prove the facts.

1. "The angles in a triangle add up to 180 degrees."
   - A person or a model could have written this. It is also a fair math fact. The sort still does not prove who wrote it.
2. "On Tuesday I lost my blue glove at soccer."
   - This has a particular day and a lost glove. It is more like a person. It is still not proof.
3. "Certainly. Water conservation is essential. Here are five key strategies for a sustainable future."
   - Smooth, generic, and padded. This is the model-like card.
4. "The Pacific Ocean is the smallest ocean on Earth."
   - Smooth and sure. It is also wrong. A model can do that. A person can too.

Close, after all four cards: "Smooth words can still be wrong." Second line, only if they want it read aloud: "A real detail is not proof."

The child is not told a tally.

### Spot the confident mistake

Fixed sample. No model call.

Label: "Sample answer. It has mistakes."

> A blue whale is the largest animal on Earth.
> It is a fish.
> Adults can be about 30 metres long.
> Blue whales lay eggs on the sea floor.

The child taps the wrong sentences.

- "It is a fish." Wrong. A blue whale is a mammal.
- "Blue whales lay eggs on the sea floor." Wrong. They give birth to live young.
- The size line is left alone. It is close enough for this lesson. Arguing about metres is not the task.
- "Largest animal on Earth" is left alone. It matches the repair.

Repair, shown after the taps, before the child can leave:

- "A blue whale is a mammal."
- "It gives birth to a live calf."
- "It does not lay eggs."

Feedback: "It sounded sure. Two parts were wrong."

Why this topic: it is school science, it is checkable, and it is not a math practice answer. The lesson must not become a way to skip a skill on `/practice`.

### Improve my prompt

Task on screen, two lines: "You are making a poster about saving water at school." "You will draw it and write the final words."

Weak prompt, shown as a quote the child should not send:

> Write my poster for me.

What is weak, in one line under it: "This asks the tool to do your work."

Better prompt, edited by the child:

> Give me three poster headlines about saving water. I will write the poster.

Word chips they can add, one at a time: "for a school hallway", "no brand names", "I will check the facts".

A finished better prompt looks like this:

> Give me three poster headlines about saving water for a school hallway.
> I will write the poster.
> I will check the facts.

The page accepts the prompt only if it keeps a limit that leaves the final poster with the student. Nothing is sent to a model on this page.

### Follow-up question ladder

The tool has just offered three headlines. The child does not need the headlines to be live. Use these fixed ones:

- "Turn it off while you brush."
- "A full tap wastes a lot."
- "Bring a bottle. Refill it."

Rungs:

| Rung | Question | Why it is here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weak | Tell me more. | It does not ask for anything the student can use or check. |
| Better | Which headline is clearest in a school hallway? | It asks for a reason and a limit. |
| Strong | What should I check before I print this? | It treats the tool as a source of claims, not of truth. |

The child taps the strong rung, then writes one follow-up of their own. A good one names a reason, a limit, or a check. Examples that pass the quiet check:

- "Which headline needs a fact I can look up?"
- "Say which one is clearest, and why, in one sentence."

Examples that get "Ask for a reason or a check.":

- "Tell me more."
- "Write the poster."
- "Just give me the answer."

### Verify with two sources

Same whale sample. Two source cards sit under it. They are short paraphrases an editor can approve before the lesson ships. They are not pasted articles. The founder picks the two real pages. Good kinds of pages: a natural-history museum, and a second reference such as a government science page or an encyclopedia. Name the source on the card. Do not invent a quotation.

Card shape:

- Source A, museum page (title filled in before build): "Blue whales are mammals. Mothers give birth to live calves."
- Source B, second reference (title filled in before build): "The blue whale is the largest animal alive. It is not a fish."

The child matches each wrong sentence to the card that disagrees. Then they tap "Both sources agree" under the repair lines from the mistake page.

The lesson does not send the child to open web search. A line for a parent, not a button that navigates away: "You can look these pages up together later."

If the two real pages disagree with this outline on a detail, fix the sample before the lesson is shown to a child. Do not ship a planted error that the sources do not clearly correct.

### My words vs AI words

Sample draft, labeled "Sample draft. Not yours yet.":

> Saving water is important for our community and our future.
> Students should take action to conserve this vital resource.
> Together, we can make a lasting difference.

The voice is smooth and empty. That is the point.

The child writes two to four sentences of their own. A strong version names a real school action, for example refilling a bottle or turning the tap off while washing hands. The outline does not force those words.

"This is mine" stays inactive while the box matches the sample, or while the box is only the sample plus a word or two. The child does not see a similarity number.

Under the box: "What did you change?" A good reflection names one cut and one added detail. Feedback is "This is in your words." There is no rubric and no grade.

## 5. Creative project options

The child picks one. Each one has a small finish that fits the last sitting. They can resume if they stop.

Shared rules:

- The tool may offer options. The child writes the final words and fills the log.
- The log is the same five lines: I asked. I kept. I changed. I checked. I wrote myself.
- The tool does not fill the log.
- Final words are the child's. A copy of a model paragraph does not pass "This is mine."
- No publish, no share link, no class feed.
- Safe output for every option: no sexual content, no private-part jokes, no slurs, no swearing, no violence, no self-harm, no real people, no classmates, no public figures, no logos, no brand names, no copyrighted characters.
- Pictures, when a picture exists, are simple illustrated drawings. Not photoreal. No text baked into the picture. The child writes the words on the page, where they can be checked.
- Until image generation is explicitly chosen, superhero and comic use reviewed sample drawings, the way the Create prototype uses files in `public/create-samples/`. The child still writes the words and the log.

When the parent toggle is on, the project step may ask the live model for three short options. The model does not return the finished piece. The request uses a server template. See section 6. Pages that teach with a planted mistake stay on fixed text, so the mistake cannot drift.

### Job ad

- **What they make:** a five-line ad for a fictional shop the child invents. Role, one task, who it is for, how to apply (a made-up shop desk, not a real address), and one line about why the work matters.
- **How AI may help:** three possible shop names, or three ways to describe one task. Not the finished ad.
- **What the child must do:** invent the shop, choose one option or reject them, write all five final lines, and drop anything that sounds like a real company.
- **Process log:** "I asked for shop names." "I kept one." "I wrote the five lines." "I checked that the shop is not real."
- **Safe output:** no real employer, no real address, no phone number, no email, no logo, no "apply on this site" link.

### Website homepage

- **What they make:** one pretend homepage for a school club or the same fictional shop. A headline, one sentence, and a button label. That is the whole page.
- **How AI may help:** three headlines, or three button labels. Not the layout code and not the finished sentence.
- **What the child must do:** choose the audience (club or shop), write the sentence, and make sure the button does not promise a real signup or a real payment.
- **Process log:** "I asked for headlines." "I wrote the sentence." "I checked that the button collects no email."
- **Safe output:** no real payment, no email box, no account form, no logo, no brand, no photo of a person. A coloured block can stand in for a picture.

### Animated superhero

- **What they make:** an original character, plus a two-frame flip. Frame one is a still pose. Frame two is a small change (an arm up, a cape shifted). The child names the character and writes one sentence about what they do.
- **How AI may help:** three original name ideas, or three pose ideas in words. Not a famous hero. Not a video.
- **What the child must do:** reject any name that copies a known character, pick the two poses, write the sentence, and say what they changed from the suggestion.
- **Process log:** "I asked for names." "I made up my own." "I described both frames." "I checked it is not a known hero."
- **Safe output:** illustrated only. No photoreal face. No real person. No logo. No weapon close-up. No blood. A conflict, if any, stays mild and off screen. No existing superhero costume. Sample art only until image generation is approved. Motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`: the flip does not autoplay.

### Comic

- **What they make:** three panels. The child writes one caption per panel. The pictures are sample drawings or, later, illustrated panels of original characters.
- **How AI may help:** a three-beat idea (start, change, end) in one sentence each. Not the finished jokes. Not the captions.
- **What the child must do:** write the three captions, decide if the pictures match the words, and change a caption when they do not.
- **Process log:** "I asked for three story beats." "I wrote the captions." "I changed panel 2. The picture did not match."
- **Safe output:** same picture rules as the superhero. No copyrighted characters. No speech that insults a group. Three panels, not a long scroll, so the page still fits.

### Poster

- **What they make:** one poster. A headline, one checked fact, and a picture area. The fact is a sentence the child can point to in the source cards or in the water example. They do not invent a statistic.
- **How AI may help:** three headlines, given the topic the child already chose. Not the fact, and not the finished poster.
- **What the child must do:** pick or rewrite the headline, write the fact in their own words, and name where they checked it.
- **Process log:** "I asked for headlines." "I wrote the fact." "I checked it against the source card." "I dropped a number the tool invented."
- **Safe output:** no brand, no logo, no scare image, no photo of a real person. Words sit in the page layout, not inside a generated image.

## 6. Filter and safety approach

The filters exist so a child cannot send a harmful prompt, and cannot see a harmful reply. They run even when the lesson is on fixed samples. A sample path still checks anything the child types.

Layers, in order:

1. **Client block list, for a fast calm stop.** A short list in the browser catches obvious sexual words and private-part slang, swear words, slurs, violence and self-harm phrases, and personal-info patterns (email, phone, street address, a PIN-like number). This is a convenience. It is not the boundary. A motivated client can skip it.
2. **Server check before any xAI call.** The route repeats the block list, normalizes case, spacing, and simple leetspeak, and runs a moderation check. If the check errors or times out, the route sends nothing and shows nothing. Fail closed.
3. **Output moderation before render.** The reply is checked before the browser is allowed to paint it. A blocked reply never flashes.
4. **Rate limits.** Proposal to confirm, not a shipped cap: 8 model calls in one sitting, and 20 calls a day per parent account. The same unfinished lesson can resume on a later day. A blocked call still counts. These sit under the monthly spend cap.
5. **No personal data in the request.** The route does not send the child's first name, grade, id, PIN, school, or the parent's email. The account has a first name. The model does not need it.
6. **Templates only.** The client sends a task id and the child's sentence, after the length cap. It cannot send a system prompt. A body that includes `system` or `prompt` is rejected. This matches the rule already written for the Create generate route on unmerged PR #23.
7. **Logging without child PII.** Do not store the prompt, the reply, or the child's words on the server. A block may store a category code and the lesson step. It must not store the text. Token counts, model name, and estimated cost can follow the `xai_usage` shape on unmerged PR #12, which already refuses to store prompts, names, PINs, and emails.
8. **Calm message.** One line, under 15 words. Input stop: "That question stays off this page. Try a school topic." Output stop: "That result stays hidden. Try a different idea." No alarm, no "policy", no quote of the blocked text.
9. **Parent visibility.** The opt-in toggle sits on the parent desk, on that child's card, off by default. When there is something to say, one quiet line: "One question was stopped." No quoted text. A student PIN session cannot open the desk. The student cookie already wins over a parent session in the same browser.

### System prompt and templates

Server-only. Version string for this outline: `ai-lesson-2026-10-22`. The client never sees the text as an editable field.

The system text should say, in short rules:

- You are a helper in a school lesson.
- Give options, limits, and checks. Do not write the student's final piece.
- Stay on the lesson task.
- No sexual content, private parts, swearing, slurs, hate, violence, self-harm, or personal data.
- No real people, brands, logos, or copyrighted characters.
- If the student asks for the finished answer, offer a plan or three options instead.
- Keep the reply short.

Example of what the server may send, after the filters pass:

> Lesson: using-ai-well. Task: poster. The student asked: Give me three poster headlines about saving water. I will write the poster. Reply with three short headlines only.

Cap the student sentence at 200 characters. Cap the reply at about 180 tokens. Reject a body over 2 KB. Those caps keep the work small on a Worker.

### How this sits next to the practice rule

The AI section is the one place a child talks to a live model. Math practice never does. `tests/policy/no-live-ai.test.ts` fails if `lib/practice`, `app/practice`, or `components` mentions `lib/xai` or `api.x.ai`. Practice keeps its own question generators. This section does not change that test.

The model call belongs in a route such as `app/api/ai/help` and a server module such as `lib/ai/`, which the policy test does not scan. The AI section has its own guardrails, listed above.

There is no separate AI plan and no price in this note. The section folds into the plan the family already has. While we test, site copy stays "Practice is free while we test." Estimates default to $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens. No product screen calls xAI on that branch. If that PR is the base, this section's live route should call `beforeXaiCall` first and `recordXaiUsage` after. If the cap refuses the call, the child sees "This help is not available right now." ### Age, COPPA, and PIPEDA

The privacy page already says accounts are created by a parent, consent comes from the parent, and AI features for children under thirteen need the parent opt-in. It is marked "Draft: to be reviewed by a lawyer." This outline does not replace that review.

The product gate is the parent toggle, off by default, one per child, on the parent desk. The child record has a first name, a grade for math, and a province. It has no birth date. Math grade does not unlock or hide an AI lesson. Each lesson shows "Suggested for age X and up" so the family can choose. The product does not treat that line as an enrolled-grade check.

The toggle screen should say what is sent (the lesson sentence, not the child's name), that a US provider may process it, and that the parent can turn it off. Turning it off locks `/ai` for that child and stops new calls. A lawyer still needs to read that wording before a live call. See the open questions.

Never ask the child for a parent email. Students do not use email. That rule is already on the privacy page and in the dashboard spec.

Dictation can send audio to the browser vendor. This lesson is typing plus Listen. No microphone in the first version.

### Workers CPU limit

The site runs on Cloudflare Workers through OpenNext. The Free plan has a tight CPU budget per request. Practice already stops a generator that draws too long (`QUESTION_DRAW_LIMIT` is 64) so one request cannot spin. AI routes need the same kind of hard stop.

Edge-safe shape:

- One model call per tap. No retry loop on the Worker.
- Cancel the upstream call at the token cap. Do not wait for a long completion.
- Moderate the short reply, then send it. Do not stream raw tokens to the screen. A stream can paint a bad sentence before the check. Buffering a 180-token reply is the compromise: the upstream call is still bounded, and the child only sees checked text.
- Do not decode images on the Worker. Do not base64 a picture through the request. Sample drawings are static files.
- Keep the block list a small string scan. Do not load a large model in the Worker to classify text.
- If image generation cannot fit the plan, superhero and comic stay on sample art.

A hard spend limit in the xAI console remains a second gate. PR #12 already says the app cap cannot stop a key used outside this Worker.

## 8. Parent guide for this lesson

This is the longer note for a parent, on their phone. Plain words. Narrow column. No scores. It describes the first lesson, Suggested for age 13 and up. The short desk note in section 7 stays as it is.

**Using AI well**

Suggested for age 13 and up.

**What your child learns**

They learn that this tool predicts likely words, and that a sure tone can still be wrong. They practice asking for options and for things to check. They mark mistakes in a sample answer. They write the final version themselves.

**Why it matters**

A copied reply is easy to forget. When a child puts an idea in their own words, they are more likely to remember it and to notice what they do not understand yet. This lesson pushes back on pasting a long answer and calling the work done. The goal is understanding, not a data dump.

**At home**

1. Close the screen and ask them to explain the idea. "Tell me in your own words."
2. Ask "How did you check that?" Wait for the source they used. A confident reply from the tool is not the check.
3. If a paragraph does not sound like them, ask "Which sentences are yours?"
4. When a reply sounds sure, ask "What could be wrong here?" Then look at one fact together.

You do not need to review every prompt. One of these questions is enough.

## 9. Age tag and prerequisites

**Age tag:** Suggested for age 13 and up

The Grade 8 anchor is the writing level of this lesson. It is not a math enrollment. Math grade does not place the lesson, hide it, or unlock it. A family self-selects from the age line on the card. Later lessons use their own lines, such as Suggested for age 15 and up on the capstone. Opening this lesson does not open those.

**Prerequisites:**

- Comfortable reading a short paragraph. The whale sample is the level of this lesson. There is no reading test and no grade gate.
- Can type a few sentences on a tablet or laptop. Listen reads the lesson aloud. The first version does not take dictation.
- A parent account already exists. The parent added the child by first name. The child signs in with that name and a PIN.
- The parent has turned AI on for this child from the parent desk. The toggle starts off. There is no second age switch.
- The child is not asked to type an email, a surname, a school, or an address.

## 11. High-school capstone

**Title:** Research, then explain

**Age line:** Suggested for age 15 and up

**One line:** Look it up, write it yourself, then say it back.

This is the next AI lesson after Using AI well. It is not a high-school math course. High-school math is not being built now. It is the next build after the AI section proves traction. The capstone uses the same AI tab, the same parent toggle, and the same filters. The age line is how a family self-selects. Math grade does not place it.

The child uses the live model to research a real topic, checks claims against real pages they open, writes a short report in their own words with citations, and ends by explaining what they learned back to the model. The model answers with questions about gaps. It never rewrites the report.

About 55 minutes. Two sittings. No clock on screen. Same device draft as the first lesson (`masterasium-ai`, up to 14 days), under a separate key. Same Listen control. Tap targets at least 48px. The main action fits 1366×768.

### Flow

**Sitting 1 — about 25 minutes**

**Topic** (`/ai/research-then-explain/topic`) — about 5 minutes

- Sees the age line and three starter topics, one screen at a time: "How do the northern lights form?" "How does a vaccine teach the body?" "Why was the CN Tower built?"
- Sees "My own topic." Their sentence goes through the filters before Continue.
- Does: picks one. A topic about a real person's private life is stopped with the usual calm line.

**Questions** (`/ai/research-then-explain/questions`) — about 8 minutes

- Sees a weak prompt: "Write my report on the northern lights." Under it: "This asks the tool to do your work."
- Does: sends a prompt such as "Give me three questions about the northern lights. I will look them up."
- The model may return only three short questions. It may not return paragraphs.
- Feedback: "Ask for questions. You will look them up."
- If they ask for the finished report, the route refuses before the call. On screen: "You write the report. Ask for a check."

**Sources** (`/ai/research-then-explain/sources`) — about 12 minutes

- Sees the citation format below.
- Does: opens three real pages themselves. For each one they type the organization or author, the page title, the year, and the page address from the address bar.
- The model may suggest a kind of source. Example on screen: "Try a space-agency page." It may not fill in the address. It does not fetch the page. The child does the reading.
- A source they did not open does not count. Button: "I opened this page."
- Need three sources, from at least two organizations.

**Sitting 2 — about 28 minutes**

**Check** (`/ai/research-then-explain/check`) — about 8 minutes

- Does: picks one claim from their notes and asks "What should I check in this claim?"
- The model may name what to compare. It may not say the claim is true.
- Does: opens one of their sources again and taps whether it agrees.
- Feedback: "You checked a real page."

**Report** (`/ai/research-then-explain/report`) — about 12 minutes

- Sees four short boxes: "My question." "What I found." "What I checked." "What is still unsure."
- Does: writes about 150 to 250 words in those boxes, in their own words, and adds the citation lines.
- This page does not call the model. "This is mine" stays quiet while the text matches an earlier model reply.
- The saved report is locked before the next step. Later replies cannot replace it.

**Explain** (`/ai/research-then-explain/explain`) — about 8 minutes

- Sees the report, read-only, and one box: "Explain it in your own words."
- Does: explains what they learned. The model may reply with up to three short questions about gaps. It must not rewrite, expand, or polish the report.
- If a reply looks like a replacement essay, it is not shown. The child sees "Here is one question to think about."
- Does: answers one of those questions in a few sentences. That answer stays separate from the report.
- Feedback: "You explained it yourself."
- Done: "You did the thinking."

Continue on the capstone home opens the first unfinished step. Leave returns to the AI lesson list.

### What the model may do

- Offer three research questions.
- Suggest the kind of source to open.
- Name what to compare in one claim.
- After the report is saved, ask up to three questions about what is unclear or missing.

### What the model may not do

- Write or rewrite the report.
- Paste a paragraph for the child to hand in.
- Invent a citation, a year, or a page address.
- Fetch the page, or browse the web on the child's behalf.
- Declare a claim true.
- Replace the child's explanation with a polished one.
- Edit the saved report.

### Citation format

Each source is four lines the child fills from a page they opened:

- Organization or author.
- Page title.
- Year.
- Page address.

After a sentence that uses the source, they add a short pointer: (Organization, Year).

Example shape, with the address left for the child to copy:

> Canadian Space Agency. What are the northern lights? 2023.
> The address from the page you opened.

A citation the child cannot open does not count. The model does not supply the address. Do not paste a copyrighted page into the report. A few words in the child's own sentence, plus the pointer, is the use.

### Filters

Section 6 applies in full. Client block list, server check before any call, output check before render, rate limits, no personal data, server templates, no prompt text in server logs, and the calm block lines.

Extra rules for this lesson only:

- There is no task that means "write the report" or "rewrite the report." Those requests are refused before the call.
- The explain task uses its own template, version `ai-capstone-2026-10-22`. The reply must be questions. A reply that restates the report as finished prose is dropped before render.
- The child's page address stays in the device draft. It is not sent to the model in this version.
- Own topics pass the same block list: sexual content and private parts, swear words, slurs, violence, self-harm, and personal information.

### Parent line for this lesson

On the desk, phone-width, when they finish:

[Name] researched a topic, checked a claim on a real page, and wrote a short report in their own words. They explained it back in their own words. The tool asked questions. It did not rewrite the report.

Ask them: "How did you check that?"
